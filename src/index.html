Where Handwired comes in.

Meet Handwired ↗

First, find your sound. Rust measures the pitch and 64 harmonics in your recording—the ingredients that give a note its character.

Then, teach the synth. Handwired builds a little trainable program that mixes those harmonics into a wave. It measures the difference from your target and calculates how each harmonic should change. As it learns, the sound gets closer.

Finally, make a melody. Choose Voice to play a loop from your actual recording, tuned to each key. Choose Learned synth to play Handwired’s harmonic approximation. Voice mode retains recorded texture and does not use Handwired for playback.

The nerdy details ＋

Handwired’s Circuit combines 64 sine waves into 256 waveform samples, computes mean squared error against a phase-aligned target, and backpropagates that error. Gradient descent updates the 64 amplitudes; locked sliders stay fixed. Rust/WebAssembly handles pitch detection and harmonic analysis. Web Audio plays the result.

The model-fit percentage measures the simplified harmonic target. The learned synth models harmonic amplitudes; speech, breath, phase, and changes over time aren’t modeled. Voice mode instead loops recorded PCM with a short crossfade and changes its playback rate. It preserves recorded texture, but shifting far from the sung pitch changes vocal color and the speed of its fluctuations. There’s no pretrained model, and no audio is uploaded.
